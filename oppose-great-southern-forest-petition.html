Stop the Great Southern Forest National Park

Local residents, recreational users, adjoining property owners, farmers and small businesses on the NSW South Coast and Shoalhaven say enough is enough.

Why we are speaking up

We are the people who live, work and recreate in and around the South Coast and Shoalhaven state forests. For generations our families have used these forests for hunting, fishing, motorbike riding, rally and recreational driving, camping, mountain biking and other traditional activities. We have looked after them as part of our local rural communities.

The National Parks Association — largely outsiders to our communities — is pushing a proposal to turn roughly 365,000 hectares of productive state forest into part of a nearly 1.2-million-hectare Great Southern Forest National Park. We already have more national park than state forest in this region. We do not want or need another one that locks us out.

What this proposal would actually mean

  • Permanent loss of public access for hunting, fishing, motorbike riding, rally car and recreational driving, camping, walking dogs and other traditional uses.
  • End of grazing, beekeeping and the ability to move recreational livestock from paddock to paddock.
  • Higher bushfire risk through the loss of active forest management and maintained access tracks.
  • Adjoining landholders left to carry the cost of new boundary fencing.
  • Risk of new biodiversity restrictions and land-tenure / rezoning changes being pushed onto neighbouring private land without compensation.
  • Serious access problems: many properties rely on tracks across Crown land or freehold boundaries. Turning this into national park removes informal access. Under NSW rules you then need a formal easement or licence, which is not automatic and can leave owners without legal right-of-way.
  • Without secure legal access, property owners can become uninsured or face much higher premiums. Adjoining a national park also commonly increases insurance costs because of bushfire risk.
  • Extra cost: national parks often charge entry fees or require paid permits just to access areas that were previously free.
  • Local small businesses and rural communities hit by reduced activity and locked-out access.

The hidden long-term cost to taxpayers and ratepayers

Carbon-credit schemes may generate revenue for the state for only about 15 years. After that, for the remaining ~85 years, taxpayers will be left to fund the higher cost of managing these areas as national park. State forests currently cost around $24 per hectare to manage. NSW national parks cost roughly $94–$98 per hectare. Local residents, ratepayers and taxpayers should not be forced to pay more while being locked out of forests we have used and cared for for generations.

What we are asking the Legislative Council to do

  • Stop the Great Southern Forest National Park proposal until a full independent social, economic and bushfire impact assessment is completed.
  • Publicly release detailed mapping and guarantee that adjoining private land will not face new biodiversity restrictions, land-tenure changes or rezoning without compensation.
  • Permanently protect existing grazing, beekeeping, livestock movement and public recreational access (including motorbike riding, rally/recreational driving, fishing, hunting, camping and walking dogs).
  • Ensure the Crown meets its boundary fencing obligations.
  • Guarantee that current road, track and bushfire management standards are maintained, and that legal right-of-way access for adjoining property owners is permanently protected so insurance cover remains valid — without forcing people to pay extra fees just to reach their own land or use the forest.

Sample letter that will be sent

To the Honourable President and Members of the Legislative Council of New South Wales. We are residents, primary producers, recreational users, adjoining property owners and State Forest users of the NSW South Coast and Shoalhaven. We write to express our strong opposition to the proposed Great Southern Forest National Park. The proposal would convert approximately 365,000 hectares of State Forest into part of a park of nearly 1.2 million hectares. This would permanently remove public access for hunting, fishing, motorbike riding, rally and recreational driving, camping and walking dogs. It would also end grazing, beekeeping and the practical ability to move livestock between paddocks. In addition, the loss of active forest management and maintained access tracks would increase bushfire risk. Adjoining landholders would be left to bear the cost of new boundary fencing. There is a clear risk that new biodiversity restrictions, land-tenure changes or rezoning could be imposed on neighbouring private land without compensation, undermining property rights and regional livelihoods. Many properties currently rely on tracks that cross Crown land or freehold boundaries. Converting this land to national park would remove informal access. Under NSW legislation, a formal easement or licence would then be required. Such approvals are not automatic. Without secure legal right-of-way, owners risk losing insurance cover or facing substantially higher premiums. Adjoining a national park commonly increases insurance costs because of bushfire risk. National parks also frequently charge entry fees or require paid permits for access that is currently free. Carbon-credit arrangements may generate revenue for the State for only about 15 years. For the remaining approximately 85 years, taxpayers and ratepayers would bear the much higher ongoing cost of national-park management. State forests currently cost around $24 per hectare to manage, while NSW national parks cost approximately $94–$98 per hectare. Local communities should not be asked to fund higher costs while being locked out of forests they have used and cared for for generations. We therefore request that the Legislative Council urge the Government to: - Stop the Great Southern Forest National Park proposal until a full independent social, economic and bushfire impact assessment is completed; - Publicly release detailed mapping and guarantee that adjoining private land will not be subject to new biodiversity restrictions, land-tenure changes or rezoning without compensation; - Permanently protect existing grazing, beekeeping, livestock movement and public recreational access; - Ensure the Crown meets its boundary fencing obligations; - Maintain current road, track and bushfire management standards; and - Permanently protect legal right-of-way access so that adjoining property owners remain properly insured, without being required to pay extra fees simply to reach their own land or use the forest. We already have more national park than State Forest on the South Coast and in the Shoalhaven. Enough is enough. We do not support a national park that locks out local rural communities. Yours sincerely, [Your name]
